This page is a citizen-held register of Canadian public commitments. Each line is a test: what counts as a pass, the date it is scored and who answers for it. The main table holds files owned by Canadian governments, legislatures, courts, regulators, Crown bodies and companies, and foreign decisions a Canadian file depends on. My own commitments, and outside files I watch, sit in short sections of their own under the same rules.

The page is dull on purpose. It does not argue, campaign or ask for anything. The essays make the arguments; this page keeps the score.

Nothing here is edited quietly. Every change is a dated entry in a public record that keeps its full history, at https://github.com/citizenheldrecord/register/commits/main. If this page and that record ever differ, the record governs.

The owner on each line is my designation of who answers for the outcome. It is not a finding of fact about any person or body.

Ways promises get eaten

Four mechanisms, set out in the essay The Reforms Were Eaten: Why the Thermometer Must Be Citizen-Held. Link: OPEN (not yet published).

  • Capture: the reform is administered by the beast it was meant to close, and becomes one more open file drawing its ration from the unfinished status.
  • Goodhart: once a measure becomes a target, the machine optimizes the number, and the published figures improve while the outcome does not.
  • The ratchet: each crisis leaves a higher baseline of spending, staffing and authority, and when the reform wave recedes the ratchet does not.
  • Time horizons and concentrated interests: those paid by the process are organized and those who pay are diffuse, and the payoff of reform arrives after the next election while the pain arrives before it.

This register records from outside the institutions it measures. It does not ask any of them to measure itself.

How to read this page

Each row is one public promise turned into a test. It says what counts as a pass, the one date it is scored, and who answers for it. Nothing on the page argues. The essays argue; this page keeps the score.

  • LIVE: the test is set and waiting for its date.
  • PROPOSED: drafted, not yet a live test.
  • PASS or FAILED: the result on the scoring date. A result is a new row; the old row is never edited. A scored row shows as CLOSED and links to the row that scored it.
  • OPEN in a cell: not yet found in a public source. It is never filled with a guess.
  • Sections: Canadian files; this desk's own commitments; outside files we watch; recorded facts and checkpoints, which are dated records rather than pass or fail tests.
  • Essay: the column that names the essay that set the test, with a link once it is published.

One worked example. Row HR5334. The test: which applied on 18 Oct 2026, a United States rate, a written waiver, or neither. Owner: US Administration / US Treasury. Date source: STATUTE: US Public Law 119-111 (H.R. 5334), sections 112, 113 and 115, deadline 18 Oct 2026. Result: SCORED 18 Oct 2026, recorded as fact. Essay: The Duty Clock Is Not a Finished War.

To check a row yourself, download the CSV below and compare it with the checksum printed on this page. The rules underneath say exactly how.

The rules

Every entry has three things: the outcome that counts as a pass, one fixed scoring date and a named owner.

On its scoring date an entry is scored PASS or FAILED. "On track" and "progressing" are not outcomes.

A FAILED entry is published within 30 days of the date. It names the layer that blocked it: title, courts, regulation, financing, statute, political choice, or another layer named in full. Its cost is priced with a source for every number, or marked OPEN.

No silent extensions. A new date is a new entry that cites the old one.

The record is permanent and append only. Nothing is deleted. A reversal or a correction goes in as a new, dated entry.

Resolved entries get three sub-scores, hype, means and cost, each MATCHED, MIXED, FAILED or OPEN. They never change the PASS or FAILED.

Every entry carries a cost to date, filled in on the scoring date from a public source showing the figures of whoever holds them (department, Crown body, company, court, foreign government, the Bank of Canada or this desk), cited with URL and date. If no public figure exists on that day, the cost sub-score is FAILED and names whoever holds the figure. "Cannot determine" is FAILED. No exemptions. The desk never estimates it. It sits inside the cost sub-score and never changes the PASS or FAILED.

Every entry carries a fingerprint, the SHA-256 checksum of its canonical text on the day it goes in, listed in a manifest kept in the public record and snapshotted on the Wayback Machine at each update. A new date, and a scoring, are each a new dated entry citing the prior entry's id and fingerprint, never an edit in place. How to recompute it, and the script, are in the rule entry of 18 October 2026.

Fingerprints use layout chr-entry-v2 from 18 October 2026, which adds the hype, means and cost sub-scores. The v1 manifest is kept and the v2 manifest is published alongside it (rule entry C).

Rules apply from their entry date forward and are never retroactive. Entries scored on or before 18 October 2026, including HR5334, keep cost to date OPEN.

Each line says why its date is what it is (a statute, a court file, a government promise, a scheduled release, a company timetable, or a reason I set and state), where the result will be read, the budget line where one is documented, and the essay that set the test. Anything not yet found is marked OPEN.

Each line also says how the money behind it is set. CASELOAD-COUPLED: the budget line grows with the volume of the problem. FIXED-OUTCOME: the money is tied to a named deliverable or milestone. TIME-LIMITED: the money sunsets on a printed end date. OPEN: no primary page shows any of these. A line is marked coupled, fixed or time limited only when a Main Estimates, Supplementary Estimates, Public Accounts, contribution agreement or Canada Infrastructure Bank financing page (or a provincial Estimates page) shows it, and that page is cited in the Money column. Everything else is OPEN, and the Money column still carries the line and amount where one was found.

Some files cannot be scored PASS or FAILED on one fixed date as written: they record a fact, a status or a condition, or carry a reading or a scoring day the main table does not use. They sit in their own section as dated records and are not counted in the main table.

Every update prints the SHA-256 checksum of the full table as a CSV file, and a Wayback Machine snapshot is taken of this page and the record. Anyone who downloads the CSV can check it against the printed checksum.

On 2 January each year the register states the files: every entry closed, open or failed, owners named, with the checksum of the whole register.

The register's only product is the score. No advocacy, no grants, no policy shop.

Rule entries

R-2026-10-18-A. Entry fingerprint

Entered 18 October 2026. From this date every entry carries a fingerprint: the SHA-256 checksum of its canonical text, taken on the day the entry goes in.

The canonical text is built the same way every time, so anyone can recompute it. The first line is the tag chr-entry-v1. Then come fifteen lines, one per field, in this order: id, section, scoring_date, sort_date, test, owner, date_source, status, measure, money, funding, argument, cost_to_date, supersedes_id, supersedes_hash. Each line is the field name, an equals sign, then the value. A field with no value is left empty after the equals sign. In each value, the text is normalised to Unicode NFC, every run of spaces, tabs or line breaks becomes one space, and spaces at the start and end are removed. The lines are joined by a single line feed, with no line feed at the end, and encoded as UTF-8. The fingerprint is the SHA-256 of those bytes, written as 64 lowercase letters and digits.

A new date is a new entry. It gets its own id and fingerprint and cites the old entry's id and fingerprint in supersedes_id and supersedes_hash. The old entry stays, with its old fingerprint.

Scoring is also a new entry. On the scoring date the result goes in as a new, dated entry with its own id and fingerprint, citing the scored entry's id and fingerprint in supersedes_id and supersedes_hash. The scored entry is not edited in place.

Every update publishes a manifest listing each entry's id and fingerprint, with the checksum of the CSV it was computed from. The manifest is kept in the public record and a Wayback Machine snapshot of it is taken at each update. Manifests are never replaced: each update adds a new one.

If an entry's fingerprint ever differs from the last manifest without a dated entry explaining why, that is itself a correction to be entered. The script that computes the fingerprints is published in the record at scripts/hash_entries.py, and anyone can run it on the downloaded CSV. The public record's commit history is already a chain of checksums; the fingerprints let a reader check a single entry without trusting this page.

This rule applies from 18 October 2026 forward. Entries scored on or before that date, including HR5334, stand as recorded.

R-2026-10-18-B. Cost to date

Entered 18 October 2026. Every entry carries a cost to date. It is filled in on the scoring date from a public source that shows the figures of whoever holds them: the department, Crown body, company, court, foreign government, the Bank of Canada or this desk. For governments that means sources such as the Public Accounts, a Departmental Results Report, the Main or Supplementary Estimates, a Parliamentary Budget Officer report, a written answer or Order Paper response, or an access to information release; for others, their own published reports, filings or records. The cell gives the amount, the source, its URL and the date it was read.

If no public figure exists on the scoring date, the cost sub-score is FAILED, and the entry names whoever holds the figure. "Cannot determine" is FAILED. There are no exemptions. The desk never estimates a cost to date. Before the scoring date, the cell reads OPEN.

Cost to date sits inside the existing cost sub-score. Like hype and means, it never changes the PASS or FAILED and never moves a cell.

This rule applies from 18 October 2026 forward and is never applied retroactively. Entries already on the register carry cost to date OPEN until their own scoring date. Entries scored on or before 18 October 2026, including HR5334, keep cost to date OPEN.

R-2026-10-18-C. Fingerprint layout version 2

Entered 18 October 2026. From this date the fingerprint uses layout chr-entry-v2. It is built exactly as the chr-entry-v1 layout in rule A, except that the first line is the tag chr-entry-v2 and three more lines follow the fifteen fields of v1, in this order: hype_score, means_score, cost_score. Every entry now carries all three sub-scores, each MATCHED, MIXED, FAILED or OPEN. An entry not yet resolved reads OPEN.

The reason for the change: the v1 layout left the hype, means and cost sub-scores outside the fingerprint, so a sub-score could have changed without any fingerprint showing it. Layout v2 closes that gap. Any column added to the register later needs a new layout and a dated rule entry; until then, that column is outside the fingerprint and the rule entry says so.

The v1 manifest of 18 October 2026 is kept unchanged in the record. From this update a v2 manifest is published alongside it, named entry-hashes-v2 followed by the update, and every build checks the data against both. No v1 fingerprint changes. The v2 fingerprints are new numbers for the same entries, not corrections.

A second update on the same day carries a letter after the date, as in 2026-10-18b, so no published file is ever overwritten.

R-2026-10-19-A. A capacity test on every levels plan

Entered 19 October 2026. Each time an Immigration Levels Plan falls due under section 94(1) of the Immigration and Refugee Protection Act, the register enters one capacity test for that plan before it is tabled. PASS: any permanent resident target above the prior plan's target for the same year is made expressly conditional on at least one published, numeric capacity measure with a stated trigger (housing starts or completions against new residents, a dated corridor or financing milestone, or a productivity or investment series). PASS-NO-INCREASE: no increase, test not triggered. FAILED: an increase with no such condition. The entry sits in the section for recorded files, because it carries a third reading and its scoring day can move to within 3 days of tabling, with a hard stop at the statutory window. The next plan's entry goes in the day after the current one is scored. A companion reading of housing completions per additional resident may be entered; its baseline is filled only from a primary source and reads OPEN until then.

R-2026-10-19-B. Transfer files: delivered against committed

Entered 19 October 2026. The register may hold entries for single international assistance transfers, read from the department's own project data. Each is scored on a fixed date on three points: disbursed at or above committed, activity status closed or in finalisation, and a published result linked to the activity. Any one missing is FAILED. Limits: at most five transfer entries open at any time; only activities with a committed amount of CAD 50 million or more and a published planned end date. The figures are the department's own and are labelled as such; the register does not present them as an audit. A loss or fraud finding abroad is never attached to a Canadian file unless a source traces it to Canadian funds. No totals, rankings or commentary on recipients.

R-2026-10-19-C. Ratchet hold

Entered 19 October 2026. An entry that scores a reduction (a headcount, a spending share, a backlog) may carry a conditional hold entry: the lower level is still met on the same series three readings later. The hold is scored only if its parent is scored PASS. If the parent is FAILED, the hold is entered VOID on the same day, not extended. Holds sit in the section for recorded files, beside the register's other conditional entries.

R-2026-10-19-D. Sunsets that fire

Entered 19 October 2026. A statutory sunset, or a statutory review deadline, in a statute already tied to an entry on this register may be entered and scored on its own date. PASS: on that date the consolidated text shows the limit unamended and the power has lapsed, or the review report has been laid. FAILED: the limit was extended or removed, or the review was not laid, cited by bill number or Journals entry. This scores the statute's own date as written. It complements the entry that scores whether a new power carries a sunset at all (BOOMBUST-SUNSET). Statutes not tied to a register entry are not added.

R-2026-10-19-E. Instrument tag

Entered 19 October 2026. Where an entry involves public money, the money column begins with INSTRUMENT: followed by GRANT, LOAN, GUARANTEE, EQUITY or IN-KIND, set only when a primary page shows it, and otherwise INSTRUMENT: OPEN. For a loan or a guarantee, the cost to date on the scoring date gives the outstanding or guaranteed amount, from the Public Accounts of Canada or the lender's own published page, separately from any grant amount. The desk never estimates it.

Labels and the fingerprint. Rules A, B and D also put EVIDENCE-HOLDER: SELF (the owner's own data) or EVIDENCE-HOLDER: INDEPENDENT (a body other than the owner) at the start of the measure column. These labels, and the INSTRUMENT tag, are written inside columns that already exist (test, measure, money, date source), so they are inside the chr-entry-v2 fingerprint. No column is added by these rules, so no new fingerprint layout is needed and chr-entry-v2 stays in use. A column added later still needs its own layout and rule entry (rule entry C of 18 October 2026).

These rules apply from 19 October 2026 forward and are never retroactive. No existing entry is changed.
