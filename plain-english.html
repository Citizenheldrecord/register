What this is

Governments make promises every week, and most are never checked. This register writes each promise down in a fixed form: what was promised, the date by which it should happen, and the person or body responsible. On that date the promise is checked against public documents and the result is recorded as passed or failed. Nothing is quietly dropped, and nothing is rewritten after the fact. The aim is simple: to give citizens back the ability to see whether their government did what it said it would do.

The register is kept by Barry Sharp's Sovereign Realism desk. The desk applies the same rules to its own commitments, and it scores itself first.

How it works

  1. Every promise has three parts. An outcome (what must be true for a pass), a date (the day it is checked) and an owner (who is accountable). If any of the three is missing, it is not a row.
  2. Every row has a fixed scoring date. The date is set when the row is entered and is not extended. On that date the row gets a recorded PASS or a dated FAILED.
  3. A failure is written up. A FAILED result gets a dated entry naming where the promise got stuck and, where it can be sourced, what the failure cost. It is published within 30 days.
  4. The record is permanent. A result is filled in once. If a result turns out to be wrong, or a government reverses itself, the original line stays and a new dated line is added below it that records the correction and its cost. Nothing already written is changed or erased.
  5. Rows stay open until they are scored. No row is retired or forgotten. Only a recorded PASS or FAILED closes it.
  6. Each resolved promise also gets three grades. Hype (did the announcement match what was delivered?), means (were the tools and money actually put in place?) and cost (what did it cost compared with what was claimed?). Each is graded MATCHED, MIXED, FAILED or OPEN, with links. These grades are filled in only after a row is resolved, and they never change the pass or fail.
  7. A yearly state of the files. Every January (the first is due 2 January 2027) the register publishes a full reading of what passed, what failed and what is still open.

How to read the table

  • ID is a short reference code so a row can be cited. It carries no meaning on its own.
  • The promise is the claim being tested, in the register's words, shortened for reading. The full pass test for each row sits in the register itself.
  • Owner is the person or body the register holds accountable. Owners are the register's designation, not a claim of fact about who is legally responsible. Where the file says an owner is unverified, the table says so.
  • Scoring date is the day the row is checked, copied from the file. Some rows are checked on an event (for example Royal Assent) or have no fixed date yet; those are listed last.
  • Status is copied from the file. LIVE, LOCKED, CONFIRMED and ENTERED all mean the row is open and waiting for its scoring date; LOCKED means the wording and date are fixed.
  • Source is a primary source link where the file gives one. A blank means the file gives no link for that row; it does not mean there is no source.
  • A scoring date already in the past with an open status means the check is due and the result has not yet been confirmed from the official record.
